# Worker s6 (round 6): scale control with Gemma 4 e4b

Started Wed 7 Oct ~19:45 ET. A diagnostic, not an e2b candidate. Questions:
1. Is e2b's hit-reading floor a scale floor? e2b reads hits at 92.2 with the gold email alone (sandwich prompt, `oracles`, FULL-0). What does e4b read?
2. Does the same engineering help a larger model by the same amount? gates − P-B for e4b vs e2b's +6.5 (FULL-0). Is e2b + gates ≥ e4b P-B?

## Setup

- **Model:** `gemma4:e4b-it-qat` (alias `mid`, digest `ee665637…`, equal to the main run's provenance; Ollama 0.34.2). Loaded only through `run2.js` (`ensureResident`) under the GPU lock.
- **Variants** (`explore2/variants/s6-scale.js`): `s6-det-pb`, `s6-det-gates`, `s6-det-oracles`. These are the frozen `explore/variants.js` `pb`, `gates` and `oracles`, each behind `det()` (`variants/i-det.js`, mode "all"). The code is model-agnostic, and the run's alias picks the model. I use my own ids so the e4b answers never mix with other workers' `i-det-*` e2b answers.
- **Runner** (`explore2/tools/s6-run.js`): calls `runExplore2({ alias: "mid" })` in chunks of at most 300 questions. Each chunk is its own `withLock` job, so other workers interleave. It refuses alias `large` and non-development sets.
- **Store safety:** `answerKey` = sha256(digest | variant@version | questionKey), and every record carries `alias`. e4b answers therefore cannot overwrite e2b answers, even under the same variant id.
- **Grading:** `cli2 grade <set> <variants>`. `gradeExplore` grades every latest answer of the named variants on the set, whatever the alias. Verdict keys are model-free (question, references, answer text, judge), so identical texts share a verdict.
- **Analysis** (`explore2/tools/s6-table.js`):
  - arms are (variant@version, alias);
  - J1, design-weighted (0.068 miss + 0.932 hit);
  - paired contrasts as per-question linear combinations, including the difference in differences for "gain of gates over P-B, e4b − e2b";
  - 95% intervals from a question-stratified paired bootstrap and a mailbox-cluster bootstrap, B = 10,000, `tools/rng.js` mulberry32.
- **Energy:** the main study's `energy-logger.js`, unchanged, records to `.data/premise2/explore/s6-energy.jsonl` for the whole session. Per-question windows are `[at − wallMs, at]`.

## Step 3: results so far

### (1) Gold-only reading: e4b vs e2b, FULL-0 (450 hits, 150 misses), J1

| arm | weighted | miss | hit | ms/q |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| e2b oracles | 91.5 | 82.0 | 92.2 | 334 |
| e4b oracles (det) | 92.9 | 90.7 | 93.1 | 551 (504 without det resets) |
| e4b − e2b | +1.4 [−1.1, 4.0] | +8.7 | **+0.9 [−1.8, 3.6] (cluster [−2.0, 4.0])** | |

- Hit flips: e4b fixes 21 and breaks 17 of e2b's hit verdicts (net +4 of 450).
- Miss-stratum questions (hard for retrieval) with the gold email given: e4b fixes 18 and breaks 5.
- **Reading the flips** (`tools/s6-flips.js`):
  - e4b's fixes are mostly wrong-fact or wrong-relation answers that it gets right: the person (Andy Lewis, Gerry Phillips, Melon, Pete Wilson), the domain (unm.edu), the list (Exhibit D changes).
  - e4b's breaks fall into two kinds. Some are new false negations ("the email does not mention/specify …"; 4 cases). Others look like J1 noise on answers that are essentially the same as e2b's ("three steps", "Candy stated", "T-shirts").
  - So e4b trades about 20 fact errors for about 5 false negations plus judge noise.

**Answer to (1):** e2b's hit-reading floor with the gold email alone is not a floor that scale lifts at the e2b → e4b step. e4b reads the gold email on hits exactly as well as e2b (92.1 vs 92.0, ±1.9). The flips are symmetric: 38 fixed, 37 broken.
- The ~8% of hits that both models miss is mostly something other than model capacity: J1 strictness, ambiguous or over-specific references, and question-email mismatches (f.md/j.md classes).
- Where e4b does read better is the gold email of *miss-stratum* questions (+6.0 on 300). These emails are harder: the retriever ranks them low, often because they are long or noisy.
- 31b cannot be tested here (it does not fit the GPU). Its P-B hit accuracy *with* four distractors (93.6, FULL-0) is already above both models' gold-only reading, so a 31b-sized model may read better. The e2b–e4b gap is nil.

- **The reading half (the sandwich prompt, which acts on hits) does not transfer.** It is an e2b-specific fix:
  - e2b + sandwich on P-B contexts reads hits at 89.3;
  - e4b with the plain T2 prompt reads them at 90.9;

### Gold-only reading, T2 vs sandwich (FULL-0 hits)

| prompt | e2b hit | e4b hit | e4b − e2b (hit) |
|---|---|---|---|
| T2 (`oracle`) | 88.9 | 92.7 | **+3.8 [0.4, 7.1]** |
| sandwich (`oracles`) | 92.2 | 93.1 | +0.9 [−1.8, 3.6] |
| sandwich − T2 | +3.3 [0.9, 6.0] | +0.4 [−1.8, 2.7] | |

**This refines (1):**
- With the plain T2 prompt, e2b's reading *is* scale-limited: e4b reads the gold email 3.8 points better.
- The sandwich prompt closes exactly that gap (e2b 92.2 vs e4b 92.7–93.1). Both models then sit at the same ~92–93 plateau, which e2b → e4b does not lift.
- So the sandwich prompt is a substitute for scale in reading, and the remaining ~7–8% of hit errors is a shared ceiling (judge strictness, references, question ambiguity) rather than a capacity floor, at least up to e4b.

## Interpretation (for the paper)

1. **Hit reading is not scale-limited at the e2b → e4b step once the prompt is right.**
   - With the gold email alone and the sandwich prompt, e2b and e4b read hits equally well: 92.0 vs 92.1, Δ +0.1 [−1.8, 2.0], 900 hits, 38 vs 37 flips.
   - With the plain T2 prompt, e4b reads 3.8 [0.4, 7.1] points better. The sandwich prompt closes exactly that gap.
   - The ~8% of hit errors left with the gold email is a shared plateau (judge strictness, references, question ambiguity), not e2b's capacity.
2. **Scale and the reading half of the engineering are substitutes.**
   - e2b → e4b buys +3.7 [1.6, 5.9] on P-B, almost all on hits (+3.9), by making the model robust to the four distractor emails:
     - distraction cost (gold-only hit − P-B hit): e2b 6.3 points, e4b 2.5 points (pooled);
     - the sandwich prompt buys e2b the same robustness;
     - it buys e4b nothing: pbs − P-B is e2b +3.2, e4b −1.0, Δ −4.2 [−8.0, −0.4].
   - The retrieval half (the gated mailbox switch with the abstention retry, acting on misses) transfers fully and is even a little larger for e4b: switch gain Δ +1.3 [0.1, 2.6]. e4b abstains on 40% of P-B misses (e2b 20%, 31b 63%), so gates' abstention retry fires on 43–47 of 150 misses (e2b 8–16).
3. **Net, the same engineering helps e4b less:** gates − P-B is +3.1 [1.1, 5.0] for e4b vs +5.7 [3.6, 7.8] for e2b. The difference is −2.6 [−5.3, 0.1] (mailbox-cluster [−5.0, −0.5]), with the same sign on FULL-0 (−2.9) and FULL-1 (−2.3).
4. **e2b + gates ≥ e4b P-B:**
   - 85.7 vs 83.7, Δ +2.0 [−0.1, 4.1] (cluster [0.2, 4.0]);
   - equal on hits (+0.0 [−2.2, 2.2]) and far ahead on misses (+30);
   - at 0.75× the wall time (746 vs 997 ms) and ~0.6× the GPU energy.
   - e4b + gates is +1.1 [−1.0, 3.1] over e2b + gates (pooled) and +1.1 [−1.6, 3.8] over 31b P-B (FULL-0).

## Caveats

- **J1 only.** J1 runs about 4 points below adjudicated scores. Both models are graded by the same judge, and verdict keys are shared for identical texts.
- **Selection bias on FULL-0.** gates was selected on FULL-0 in phase 1, so e2b's FULL-0 gates gain carries some winner's-curse inflation. FULL-1 was never used for selection and shows the same direction: e2b +5.0, e4b +2.6.
- **The engineering was tuned on e2b.** The sandwich prompt was found by fixing e2b's failures, so a smaller gain on another model is partly expected. That is the substantive point: it compensates for a small-model weakness.
- **det vs non-det.** The e2b arms are the stored non-det runs (FULL-0 pb, gates, oracles; FULL-1 gates, oracles) plus the det `s6-det-pb` on FULL-1. The e4b arms are all det. On FULL-1, e2b `gates` and `i-det-gates` score the same (84.6, 29.3 / 88.7), so det does not move accuracy levels. Cross-model pairs are never byte-identical anyway.
- **31b.** 31b P-B exists only on FULL-0 (round-0 overlap run, timing invalid). 31b gold-only reading cannot be measured on this 8 GB GPU.
- **Cost.** GPU time ≈ 78 min of the 90 min budget, excluding queue waits. J1: ~3,350 calls, ≈ $0.06.
