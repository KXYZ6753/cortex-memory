# v6: an independent encoder as an answer selector on hits (round 6) — **aux**

Prefix `v6`. Code: `explore2/variants/v6-common.js` (encoders and scores), offline tools `explore2/tools/v6-*.js`.

**Scope flag (aux).** The project rule is "e2b is the only generator; retrieval helpers allowed". An encoder that *chooses among e2b's own answers* is neither a generator nor a retrieval helper: it is a new category. Every number, table and variant in this note is labelled **aux**. Whether an aux selector counts in the paper is **Kerem's decision**. In every policy here the final answer is one of e2b's own candidate texts; the encoder only chooses.

**Question.** e2b's answers under different prompts or paths contain the right answer far more often than any single system does: the union of 4 prompts reaches about 97% on hits (n.md), and oracle selection among x1's candidates is worth about +4 weighted (l.md). Every e2b-internal selector failed, because e2b's judgments are correlated with its own reading errors: agreement, logprob, YES/NO verification, pairwise choice and lexical grounding. Does a non-generative encoder, trained elsewhere, carry a selection signal that e2b lacks?

## 0. Setup

- **Encoders (CPU, `@huggingface/transformers` 4.2.0, ONNX int8, under `.data/models`):**
  - **QA, primary:** `onnx-community/roberta-base-squad2-ONNX`, an extractive reader trained on SQuAD 2.0 (deepset roberta-base-squad2), 123 MB.
  - **NLI:** `Xenova/DeBERTa-v3-base-mnli-fever-anli`, 242 MB.
  - `onnx-community/deberta-v3-large-squad2-ONNX` int8 (522 MB) was downloaded and dropped. Its int8 export is broken: through transformers.js's own QA pipeline it answers "Who sent the email?" with a date span, at probability 0.002. The files were deleted. Downloads totalled 887 MB, under the 1 GB budget.
- **QA read.** The question is run over each evidence email (the exact text e2b sees: Subject/Sender/Recipients/File header plus body) in 384-token windows with a 128-token overlap, up to 12 windows per email. The top 20 spans per window (start + end logit, ≤ 30 tokens) are cached per (question, email). This costs about 57 ms per window and 0.14 s per email on 6 threads when the machine is quiet, and about 100 ms per window under the round-6 CPU load. Gates' 5-email context is about 0.7–1.3 s.
- **Candidate scores** (`qaScores`). The 20 best distinct spans over the whole evidence set get a softmax distribution:
  - **`ef`** (primary, fixed before any evaluation): the expected share of a span's content words that the candidate contains, Σ p(s)·cover(s, c).
  - `ef5`: the same over the top 5 spans.
  - `max`: the best span logit that the candidate covers ≥ 50%.
  - `top1`: the candidate's cover of the single best span.
- **Baselines on the same evidence:**
  - `lex`: l's lexical grounding, the share of the answer's novel words that appear in the evidence.
  - `prox` (a stronger lexical baseline I added): the max over 60-word windows of (share of the answer's novel words) × (0.5 + share of the question's words), which rewards an answer that sits next to the question's words.
  - `len`: answer length.
- **Evidence is always deployable:** the emails the candidates were read from, or the commit-check YES email. It is never the gold email (l.md: gold evidence inflates every selector).
- **Pools** (`v6-pools.js`): alias `small` only. FULL-0 also holds 600 answers from the 31b `large` alias, and `l-lib.js`'s loader does not filter alias; this did not affect l's numbers because l never used FULL-0. Verdicts are joined by text, as in l.
  - **A, same context.** Gates' answer plus every stored one-shot reader with another prompt on exactly gates' first context: T2 (gate/gatea, pb when its context equals gates'), o1/o2/o4/o5/o7/o9, gatesi/m/f, demos b-g2c/b-g4f/b-g4s, thread labels c-gT/c-gC, u-gcad, u-grep, i-det-gates, n-lp0. Evidence = gates' 5 context emails, which every candidate read. Sets: FULL-0, S300-1, S300-2, S100-7/8/9 (alternatives exist there), plus FULL-1, S300-3 and S100-2 (gates only). There are 173 mixed hits and 67 mixed misses (some candidate right, some wrong). The T2 alternative exists on 987 hits.
  - **B, det family.** Det runs of gates, x1, q1, q2, lite, plus the x1-family commit answer (gates' prompt on W0). Evidence = union of the runs' contexts and read emails, or the commit-check YES email. Sets: S300-1..5, FULL-2, FULL-3 (1,900 hits). There are 125 mixed hits and 225 mixed misses.
- **Statistics.** Pairwise accuracy is over (right, wrong) pairs within a question, ties ½, with a question-clustered bootstrap. Policy Δs use a question-stratified paired bootstrap and a mailbox-cluster bootstrap. B = 10,000 with mulberry32 (`tools/rng.js`).

## 1. Pool A: does the QA encoder see something e2b and word overlap do not? (`v6-eval.js pairs A`)

**aux.** 173 mixed hits (1,615 pairs) and 67 mixed misses (649 pairs); evidence = gates' 5 emails.

| score | hits pairwise [95% CI] | hits, hard pairs (both answers ≥ 80% grounded; 943 pairs, 138 q) | misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **QA `ef`** | **64.7 [58.5, 70.7]** | **64.7 [57.9, 71.6]** | 62.0 [54.2, 68.9] |
| QA `ef5` | 62.7 [57.4, 68.0] | 61.0 [55.4, 66.9] | 55.2 |
| QA `max` | 57.5 | 54.0 | 56.9 |
| QA `top1` | 58.8 | 56.6 | 52.2 |
| `lex` (l's lexical grounding) | 48.5 [43.3, 54.0] | 44.9 [39.6, 50.4] | 50.1 |
| `prox` (lexical, question-proximity) | 56.2 [50.2, 62.2] | 50.4 [43.6, 57.3] | 48.5 |
| `len` (longer answer) | 54.8 | 58.7 | 54.9 |
| *for reference, l.md: e2b verifier, hard pairs, gold / YES email* | | *52.2 / 50.6* | |

- **There is a real, independent signal.** On hard pairs, where both answers are drawn from the evidence, the QA reader ranks the right answer higher 64.7% of the time. e2b's own verifier was a coin flip there (l.md), and word overlap is a coin flip or worse.
- **It is not just length.** Pairwise `ef` by length bucket (`v6-lencheck.js`):
  - similar length (0.8–1.25×): 63.1 [56.4, 69.8], 735 pairs;
  - right answer longer: 77.5;
  - right answer shorter: 52.4.

  So `ef` carries a length bias (a longer answer covers more spans) on top of a signal that holds at equal length.
- **Parent vs alternative** (`v6-disc.js`; gates' answer vs each discordant same-context alternative; AUC of score(alt) − score(gates) for "the alternative is right"; 382 hit pairs, 173 q, gates right in 221):

| score | hits, all | hits, gates unsure (logprob < −0.1; 143 pairs, 48 q) | hits, gates confident (114 pairs, 62 q) | misses (153 pairs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| QA `ef` | **0.676 [0.596, 0.750]** | 0.737 [0.583, 0.875] | 0.639 | 0.653 |
| `prox` | 0.632 [0.546, 0.713] | 0.591 | 0.591 | 0.532 |
| `lex` | 0.503 | 0.415 | 0.492 | 0.503 |
| `len` | 0.512 | 0.450 | 0.567 | 0.568 |

  Restricting the evidence to gates' first email or first two emails leaves `ef` at 0.670 and 0.687 (`v6-disc.js all 1|2`).

- **What the encoder sees** (`v6-show.js`; the first discordant alternative per mixed hit, 173 questions). The margin toward the right answer is:
  - decisive and right (> 0.1) on 40;
  - decisive and wrong (< −0.1) on 14;
  - undecided (|d| ≤ 0.1) on 119, where the reader's top spans are in neither answer or in both.

  So when it is decisive it is right 74% of the time, but it is decisive on only about 31%.
  - **Its wins are real reading wins**, often relation errors e2b makes from the right email:
    - "Who requested that the new gas daily index product be mapped in TEST?": the top span is "Andy Lewis", which only the right answer names; the wrong one says Chris Walker requested it.
    - "fraternity days", "slight changes", "received no reply".
  - **Its losses:**
    - the extra-detail answer that contains a distractor span ("Tom Patrick");
    - a mail-encoding artefact ("Coll=ege Station");
    - several plausible spans in the same email.
- **Arithmetic of the policy.** With about 31% coverage at 74% precision and the parent right on a share π of the d discordant hits, the expected gain is d·0.31·[(1−π)·0.74 − π·0.26].
  - Gates + T2 (d ≈ 10.5%, π ≈ 0.64): about +0.3 hit points, which is what the simulation shows.
  - An alternative with π ≈ 0.42 and d ≈ 6.5% (o4 on S300-1/2): about +0.6.
  - Reaching +1.0 needs a more decisive reader (higher coverage) or a larger discordant pool with a weaker parent.


## 2. Pool A: deployable policies (`v6-sim.js`)

**aux.** Keep gates' answer, and switch to an alternative only if score(alt) − score(gates) > m. The margin is chosen leave-one-set-out: on the other sets, by hit Δ, then applied to the held-out set. Only verdict-discordant questions can move, and those are all read, so the simulation is exact on stored candidates.

| alternatives (gate) | hits | oracle | LOSO Δ hit points [95% CI] (mailbox-cluster CI) | hit flips | misses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T2 prompt on gates' context (none) | 987 | +3.75 | **+0.41 [−0.41, 1.22]** (−0.29, 1.09) | +10/−6 | +3/−5 |
| T2 (only when gates is unsure; conf known for ~60%) | 987 | +1.22 | +0.30 [−0.20, 0.81] | +5/−2 | +1/−1 |
| every stored same-context prompt (none) | 1,699* | +3.53 | +0.06 [−0.65, 0.77] | +19/−18 | +13/−9 |
| every stored same-context prompt (unsure) | 1,699* | +1.18 | +0.18 [−0.12, 0.47] | +5/−2 | +5/−2 |

\* includes sets with gates only (FULL-1, S300-3, S100-2), where nothing can change.

- The best in-sample cells are T2 with `max` at m = 2 (+5/−0 hits, +0.51 [0.10, 1.01]) and `ef` at m = 0.4 when unsure (+3/−0). Both are precise and rare, and each is 1 of about 25 cells.
- **Why a 0.68-AUC signal does not pay:** on discordant hits gates is right 58–64% of the time, and `ef` prefers gates' answer in about 75% of those pairs whatever the truth.
  - It picks T2 in 35% of the pairs where T2 is right and in 19% of the pairs where gates is right.
  - At m = 0 that is +13/−13.
  - With one alternative the prize is +3.75 hit points. A selector at AUC 0.68 against a 60/40 prior keeps about a tenth of it.

## 3. A hypothesis from pool A, and its pre-registered det test (written Wed 7 Oct ~20:50 ET, before any `p6-e4` answer existed)

**aux.** The prior matters as much as the encoder. A selector at AUC ≈ 0.68 pays only when the alternative is right on about half of the discordant questions (`v6-oracle.js`):

| parent + alternative (S300-1, S300-2, FULL-0) | hits | oracle | parent right on discordant hits |
|---|---|---|---|
| gates + T2 | 843 | +4.03 | 59/93 (63%) |
| gates + o4 (S300-1, S300-2 only) | 400 | +3.75 | 11/26 (42%) |
| gates + T2 + o4 | 393 | +6.11 | 31/55 (56%) |
| x1 + its commit answer | 760 | +1.45 | 13/24 |

On gates + o4 (stored, not det; S300-1 + S300-2, 400 hits), comparing `v6-sim.js o4 none <score>` at m = 0 with always taking o4:

| policy | Δ hit points [95% CI] | hit flips | miss flips |
|---|---|---|---|
| **QA `ef`** (m = 0; leave-one-set-out also picks 0) | **+1.50 [0.25, 3.00]** | +7/−1 | +2/−0 |
| always o4 | +1.00 | +15/−11 | +6/−5 |
| `prox` | +1.00 [−1.00, 3.25] | +11/−7 | +1/−3 |
| `len` | +0.50 | +5/−3 | |
| `lex` | +0.00 | +6/−6 | |

This is only 400 hits and 8 switches, and o4 was picked after its prior was seen; the prior is itself computed from verdicts, and "always o4" is already +1.0 here. p6.md re-scores o4 vs gates on these two sets as +0.97 [−1.40, +3.33], the same scale as u's padding placebo. **So this is a hypothesis, not a result.**

**Pre-registered test (exact, offline, det vs det).** p6 runs `p6-e4` (gates behind det with every answer prompt in o4's layout) on S300-1, S300-4, S300-5, FULL-2 and FULL-3. Under det every call starts from the same cache state, so `p6-e4`'s text is what a live `v6-go4` would generate.

- **Rule R1:** answer = the `p6-e4` text if ef(`p6-e4`) − ef(`i-det-gates`) > 0; otherwise the `i-det-gates` text.
- **Evidence:** the union of both runs' read emails (normally gates' first 5-email context).
- **QA model and score:** fixed as in §0 (top 20 spans, T = 1).
- **Sets:** S300-4, S300-5, FULL-2, FULL-3, which are 1,300 hits. S300-1 is reported separately, because o4's hypothesis came from S300-1 and S300-2.
- **Comparisons:** Δ vs `i-det-gates` (the parent) and vs `p6-e4` alone, on hits, with question-stratified and mailbox-cluster bootstraps.
- **Bar:** Δ vs the parent ≥ +1.0 hit points with the lower bound > 0, *and* Δ vs `p6-e4` alone > 0. Without the second condition, the encoder adds nothing beyond the prompt.
- Margins other than 0 are reported as exploratory.
- **Outcome rule:** only if R1 passes is the live variant `v6-go4` (`variants/v6-sel.js`) run on H6-D (≤ 2 variants). If it fails, nothing goes to the GPU.

## 4. NLI cross-encoder (`v6-nli.js`, `v6-nlieval.js`, `v6-combo.js`, `v6-combosim.js`)

**aux.** Pool A mixed hits, 173 questions, the same pairs as §1. The premise is the 3 email chunks (320 tokens) with the most overlap with question + answer. Score = max over chunks of logit(entail) − logit(contradict). DeBERTa-v3-base int8 runs at about 190 ms per pair on 6 threads under load.

| score | pairwise | hard pairs | AUC, gates vs alternative (382 pairs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| NLI, hypothesis = answer | 50.7 [45.5, 55.9] | 49.5 | 0.529 |
| NLI, hypothesis = question + answer | 59.6 [53.7, 65.6] | 58.2 [50.3, 65.5] | 0.658 |
| QA `ef` (§1) | 64.7 | 64.7 | 0.676 |
| **QA + NLI (rank sum, no fitted parameters)** | | | **0.723** |

- **NLI on the answer alone is a grounding check**, like e2b's verifier (l.md), and is a coin flip on hard pairs. Adding the question gives it a real but weaker signal than the reader.
- **The two encoders are independent of each other:** the rank correlation of their margins on the 382 pairs is −0.004. Combined they are better than either alone.
- **Policy, exploratory, in-sample** (15 cells: 3 scores × 5 thresholds). Combined margin = ef margin / 0.35 + NLI(q+a) margin / 1.71, with the SDs taken over gates + T2's discordant hit pairs.

| alternative | hits | rule | Δ hit points [CI] | hit flips |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gates + T2 | 987 | QA only, best threshold | +0.30 | +4/−1 |
| | | NLI only, > 1 SD | +0.71 [0.10, 1.32] | +8/−1 |
| | | **QA + NLI, > 1 SD** | **+0.81 [0.20, 1.52]** | +10/−2 |
| | | QA + NLI, > 0.5 SD | +0.81 [−0.10, 1.72] | +15/−7 |
| gates + o4 | 400 | QA + NLI, > 1 SD | +1.25 [0.25, 2.50] | +5/−0 |
| | | QA + NLI, > 0 SD | +2.00 [0.50, 3.75] | +10/−2 |

- **Leave-one-set-out** (threshold chosen on the other sets; gates + T2, 987 hits): QA + NLI is **+0.20 [−0.51, 0.91]** (+8/−6). The best threshold moves across sets (0.5 to 1.5 SD), so the in-sample +0.81 at a fixed 1 SD is partly threshold luck. The honest range for gates + T2 + both encoders is about +0.2 to +0.8 hit points, below the +1.0 bar.

**Secondary rule R2 for the det test (pre-registered here, Wed 7 Oct ~21:15 ET, before any `p6-e4` answer existed).** Switch to `p6-e4`'s text iff (ef(o4) − ef(gates)) / 0.35 + (NLI_qa(o4) − NLI_qa(gates)) / 1.71 > 1.0. The NLI premise is the 3 best-overlap chunks of the union of both runs' read emails. Same sets, bar and comparisons as R1. R1 stays the primary rule; R2 is reported as secondary.
- Extra live cost of R2: about 2 × 3 NLI pairs ≈ 1.1 s on questions whose texts differ (about half).

## 7. Conclusions (aux)

- **An independent encoder does carry some selection signal that e2b and word overlap lack, but it is weak and it does not pay.**
  - On pool A (same-context prompt variants; 173 mixed hits), the extractive QA reader ranks the right answer above the wrong one 64.7% of the time.
  - It does so on hard pairs too (64.7%, both answers grounded in the evidence). There e2b's own verifier (l.md) and lexical grounding are coin flips (≈ 51% / 45%).
  - The signal survives length control (63.1% at similar length), and an NLI cross-encoder (question + answer) adds an uncorrelated second signal (combined AUC 0.723).
  - When the reader is decisive (31% of pairs) it is right 74% of the time, and its wins are real reading wins (relation errors such as requester vs sender).
- **It fails as a selector:**
  - on the det family (pool B: 55%, coin flip);
  - and on fresh det data for the most favourable alternative (gates vs o4 layout: AUC 0.522 on 85 discordant hits).
- **Policy results, best first** (Δ vs the parent in hit points; parent kept unless a margin):

| configuration | evidence level | Δ [CI] | hit flips |
|---|---|---|---|
| gates + T2 + QA, ≥ 900 hits | offline, LOSO margin | +0.41 [−0.41, 1.22] | +10/−6 |
| gates + T2 + QA + NLI | offline, LOSO | +0.20 [−0.51, 0.91] | +8/−6 |
| (gates + T2 + QA + NLI at a fixed 1 SD) | in-sample | +0.81 [0.20, 1.52] | +10/−2 |
| x1 + its commit answer + QA | in-sample, 1 of 45 cells | +0.53 [0.11, 0.95] | +13/−3 |
| **gates + o4 layout + QA (R1), pre-registered** | **1,300 fresh det hits** | **+0.08 [−0.62, 0.77]** | **+11/−10** |
| gates + o4 layout + QA + NLI (R2), pre-registered | 1,300 fresh det hits | +0.15 [−0.31, 0.62] | +6/−4 |

- **Why the signal does not pay:**
  - the prize per alternative is small (oracle +1.5 to +4 hit points);
  - the parent is right on 55–64% of the discordant hits;
  - the reader is decisive on only about a third of them.

  Expected gain ≈ discordance × coverage × (precision − prior effects), at most +0.3 to +0.6 hit points with this encoder, which is what was measured.
- Only a stronger reader could change this. DeBERTa-v3-large SQuAD2 (F1 ≈ 91 vs 83) exists only as a broken int8 export within the 1 GB budget, and it would be about 4× slower on CPU, pushing a live variant past the 3,243 ms cap.
- **Promotion: none.** Nothing is a candidate for H6-C confirmation. No v6 variant ran on H6-D (0 of 2 slots used).
- **Cost:**
  - `v6-o4` (the alternative alone): 725 ms and 2.0 calls (1 det reset + 1 answer).
  - A live `v6-go4` would be about 0.8 s (gates) + 0.73 s (o4 layout) + the QA encoder (0.15–0.3 s per email × 5 emails on the ~78% of questions whose texts differ). That is ≈ 2.4–2.9 s and 4.1 calls under the round-6 CPU load. `v6-go4n` adds about 1–1.3 s of NLI on those questions, which puts it at or over the cap.
- **Scope (aux):** whether an encoder used as an answer selector is admissible in the paper is Kerem's decision. With these results it is moot for the leaderboard, but the pool A finding (an independent reader sees 64.7% on pairs where e2b's verifier sees 51%) may be worth a sentence in the discussion of why e2b-internal selection fails.
