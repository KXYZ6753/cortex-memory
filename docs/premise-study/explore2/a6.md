# a6: error-class surgery on hits (round 6)

Prefix `a6`. Code: `explore2/variants/a6-surgery.js`; offline tools `explore2/tools/a6-*.js` (`a6-lib.js` loaders + bootstrap, `a6-inventory.js`, `a6-tax.js`, `a6-patterns.js`, `a6-gold-eval.js`, `a6-check.js`).
Question: find the largest classes of wrong hit answers with a mechanical cause; give only those questions a targeted treatment; every other prompt stays byte-identical to the det parent.

Note on data hygiene: the "test:" prefix of a questionKey is EnronQA's own split, not the study's TEST (17,234 pool records carry it). a6-lib keeps only answers and verdicts whose questionKey is an exploration-pool record.

## 1. Fresh taxonomy (dev sets S300-4, S300-5, FULL-2, FULL-3; 1,300 hits; det answers)

Systems on these 1,300 hits (J1): det gates 88.3, det x1 87.9, det q1 88.5, det lite 87.8. Gold-only oracle: `a6-det-oracle` (det, sandwich prompt, gold email alone; run for this study on all four sets): 91.3.

(share = of all 1,300 hits; oracle = the same question read from the gold email alone.) Context-addressable (oracle right): WE almost entirely, PART and WRONG about half, OMIT and NAME rarely (reading limits / judge strictness), TRUNC never (the oracle is cut too). Read by hand (≈ 120 wrong answers of gates and q1): PART answers almost always address every part; one part is wrong, under-detailed or read from another email ("Some bondholders may consider $50 million a sum worth fighting for" without the bankruptcy concern; "addressed to Francisco Pinto" for "Francisco Pinto Leite"); three of gates' PART cases are list answers cut at 160 tokens. OMIT is mostly single-part questions whose gold carries one more detail (city/ZIP of an address on the next line, "Inc.", "Portland, OR 97204", a price). The long tail is real: answer-shape patterns with a mechanical cause are each ≤ 0.5% of hits (`a6-patterns.js`, 1,300 dev hits, gates): truncation 3 (all wrong), hedges 10 (3 right), an email address given for a person 10 (7 right), Lotus routing suffixes 7 (5 right), "you/your" for a person 89 (88% right, same as average), relative dates ("tomorrow", "yesterday") on when-questions 18 (13 right). Even fixed perfectly, all of them together are worth < 1 hit point.

The single largest structural class is the genuine two-part question: 7.4% of hits, read 12–15 points worse than the rest by every system; ~23 wrong per system per 1,300 hits (≈ 1.8 hit points). On all nine dev sets (2,800 hits; `splitParts` fires on 8.4%): x1 81.6 / q1 81.0 / lite 79.7 on split vs 89.2–89.6 on the rest. Gold-only reading is 85.4% on them (vs 91.8% on the rest; old sets' stored `oracles`: 87.3% vs 92.2% on 134 split hits): about 6 points of the class's gap are reading, about 9–10 points are context (the five-email prompt).

`isMultiPart` (s.md) over-counts: 270 hits of 1,300, but 174 of them are single questions with a preamble ("According to Brenda Meisner, why ..."); on those, accuracy is normal. `splitParts` merges such preambles into the next part, does not split relative clauses (", which ...", ", where ..."), and needs ≥ 3 words per part.

Who-questions and header-field questions are the other low cells (77–82%), with the oracle 5–7 points higher, but the old sets' stored oracle is not better than the systems there (who: oracle 76.0 vs q1 74.0 on S300-1/2/3), and earlier rounds found their errors to be relation inversions at oracle level (n.md, c.md, s.md clzW +3/−2). A "top-1 email alone" read for these cells, simulated with oracle verdicts (`top1sim`), is not better than the systems on the old sets (q1 who 62.0 vs 74.0). Not pursued.

## 2. Treatment design: multi-part questions only

Trigger (deployable, no gold): `splitParts(question)` returns ≥ 2 parts. It fires on 7–8% of hits (and a similar share of misses). Parent answer abstained → no action.

Hypothesis from the PART reading: the prompt's length rule ("Answer every part of the question in one or two sentences") gives two-part questions half the room per part, and the second part is answered thinly or from the wrong message. Two treatments, both re-answering the parent's final reading context (same emails, same order) only on split questions:
- **plist**: one call; the length rule reads "one or two sentences per part" and the final question lists the parts ("The question has 2 parts: 1. … 2. … Answer every part.").
- **dec**: one call per part; the sandwich prompt with the part as the question and the full question named once as context ("(This is part 2 of 2 of the question: "…")"); the part answers are joined. Any part abstaining → keep the parent's answer.
- (gold-only harness only) **decs**: the part alone as the question; and **pad**, the placebo.

Parents: det gates (`i-det-gates@2`) and det q1 (`q-det-q1@1`). For q1 the final context is its stored readPaths (W0 on commits, [E, W0 top 4] on m2 recoveries, g5's reads on handovers).

Mechanics. `a6-g-dec/plist` and `a6-q-dec/plist` are the deployable variants (parent run unchanged, then the form on split questions). For development they are measured as replays, `a6-rg-*` / `a6-rq-*` (z-replay.js pattern): unsplit questions return the stored det parent answer; split ones run only the form, behind det, over the stored final context. Behind det this is the real variant's answer exactly: `a6-check.js` (stub generator) shows the a6-g-* prompt log identical to i-det-gates' on every unsplit question (262/262 S300-4, plus S300-5) and, on split ones, gates' log followed by the form's prompts each holding exactly gates' final context (38 + 28 fired, 0 bad); the stored first contexts of i-det-gates equal the recomputed ones on all 184 split questions of the four new sets. Cost of the real variant = parent's wall/calls + the form's (stored per answer as parentWallMs / parentCalls). `tools/a6-batch.js` runs several replay jobs under one GPU lock (run2.js's loop and ctx), so short jobs do not each wait a full queue turn.

### 3e. Decision rule for H6-D (written Thu 03:35 ET, before the top2x and S300-2/3/FULL-1 replays were seen)

- Package = TRUNC repair + top2 on split questions with the attribution guard (§3d), plus top2 on each further context-sensitive cell (who, header-field, when; `cellOf`) whose top2x replay on the dev sets is net positive on hits (+2 or better) and not net negative on misses, per parent.
- The package goes to H6-D for a parent only if, composed offline over every dev set with that det parent (gates: S300-1..5, FULL-1..3; q1: S300-1..5, FULL-2/3), its hit Δ is ≥ +1.0 with the question-bootstrap lower bound > 0, within the cost cap. Otherwise no H6-D run.
- H6-D runs are replays on the lead's stored det parents (`a6-rg-pkg`, `a6-rq-pkg`; identical to the live `a6-g-pkg` / `a6-q-pkg` behind det), queued early because of the queue length and gated by `"go": true` in `variants/a6-final.json` at the moment the job starts.

## 4. Conclusions

1. **Taxonomy.** On 1,300 fresh dev hits the wrong answers (11.5–12.2% of hits per system) are a long tail. Classes with a mechanical cause are small: truncation 0.2%, hedges 0.5%, email address / Lotus suffix for a person ≤ 0.5%, relative dates on when-questions ~0.4%, partial names 0.4–0.5%. Wrong-email reads are 1.3–1.8% (the oracle fixes most of them, but nothing deployable identifies the right email: header-mismatch triggers fire on 9% of hits at 82% accuracy, the attributed email is the gold in 83 of 117). The one structural class is the genuine two-part question: 7.4% of hits, read at 74–77% by every system (89% on the rest), oracle 85.4%.
2. **Multi-part surgery by reformulation does not work.** Decomposing into one call per part, or listing the parts with a per-part length rule, re-rolls e2b's answers on the class at a coin flip: gold-only (96 split hits) decs +8/−8, dec +4/−5, plist +2/−2; end to end dec −0.13 (gates) / −0.11 (q1), plist −0.40 / −0.32. e2b answers the whole question again in each part call and picks up details from the other emails. This extends s.md's cloze result: a 2B reader's multi-part errors are not a prompt-format problem.
3. **Fewer distractors for the class helps a little, consistently.** Answering split questions from the first 2 emails of the parent's own final context (same prompt, no new wording), unless the parent's answer came from email 3–5: gates +11/−6, q1 +10/−4 on fired hits (pooled +0.21 / +0.32 hit points). It is the only class-targeted change in this study that is positive on both parents; it does not generalise to the who / header-field / when cells (gates on the three sets not used to pick the cells: −4 net; q1: −5 net), so the context effect is specific to two-slot questions.
4. **TRUNC repair is free and certain but tiny**: +5/−0 on gates, +2/−0 on q1 (≈ +0.1–0.2 hit points); every deployed pipeline should re-ask a 160-token-cut answer with a longer budget.
5. **Best variant: `a6-g-pkg`** (det gates + top2 on split questions with the attribution guard + TRUNC repair; config `a6-final.json`): +0.43 [+0.04, +0.81] hit points over 2,350 dev hits (flips +16/−6, misses −0.11), 838 ms, 1.12 real calls. On q1 (`a6-q-pkg`): +0.42 [0.00, +0.84] (+12/−4, misses +0.25), 2,483 ms, 6.80 calls. Below the +1.0 bar: **not a candidate** for H6-D / H6-C. It is a clean, zero-risk add-on (it changes ~8% of answers, all other prompts byte-identical) if the lead wants one; expected value ≈ +0.4 hit points.
6. Why the bar is out of reach for class surgery: every class above is ≤ 2% of hits except the two-part questions (7.4%), and within that class even the gold-only reading is only 85%. Fixing the whole context-attributable part of the class (oracle − system ≈ 9 points × 7.4%) would be worth ≈ +0.7 hit points; top2 recovers about a third to a half of it (+0.21 gates, +0.32 q1).
